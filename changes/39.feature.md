The `rabi` step runs as a `RabiNode` (`FixedFluxNode`): one Rabi amplitude sweep with two analyses, chosen per step (QHC-1806).

- The QProgram is the shared `rabi_amplitude` experiment from `qili-qprograms` (QHC-1914), consumed as a dependency rather than copied: it plays the calibrated `pi-<operating_point>` pulse, normalised to unit amplitude, through a gain sweep that averages inside the hardware loop, and reads the `readout-<operating_point>` pulse, its weights and the drive/readout IFs from the calibration file. The node builds it and runs it directly, as `t1` does. The operating point's LOs, readout attenuation and drive source come from the shared `_set_operating_point_hardware` (`drive_power` for a Rohde & Schwarz source). A QCM-RF drive runs at `drive_gain` 1, and any other value fails the step before the hardware is touched: Qblox multiplies the sequencer's static gain into the swept `set_awg_gain`, so the fitted amplitude would not hold at gain 1.
- Step keys follow the qili-qprograms argument names: `drive_amplitude_sweep` (an `np.linspace` window), `averages`, `relax_duration` and `readout_ringup_time`; `min_r2` defaults to 0.9. The example tree's `rabi` defaults, ADR-0003's tree and the parser test fixture are updated to match. A tree that still sets `amplitude_sweep` parses, but every rabi step then fails with "sets no 'drive_amplitude_sweep'".
- `readout_levels` writes the |0>/|1> readout levels, with their uncertainties, onto the operating point, tagged with the `readout-<operating_point>` pulse they were taken with. `pi_amplitude` (the default analysis) writes the fitted pi amplitude into the `pi-<operating_point>` pulse in `Calibration.waveforms`: the pulse `rabi_amplitude` played, at the fitted gain (`qili_qprograms.waveforms.scaled(unit_amplitude(pulse), pi_amplitude)`), so any pulse shape works and its sign is kept. Both pulses must already exist (they come with the manual calibration): the node seeds none, and a missing one fails the step before any hardware is set.
- `RabiAmplitudeModel` fits the rotated signal to `FittingClass.cosfunc` with its phase fixed at 0: the rotation is linear in the drive amplitude and starts from |0> at zero drive. The amplitude is signed, since `rotate_iq` may put |0> at the top or the bottom of the trace, and the fit starts from `FittingClass.oscillation_initial_guess`, a new shared helper: it scans the angular frequency, fitting the offset and the cosine by linear least squares at each, and keeps the best. An FFT peak could not resolve the example tree's window, which holds about 1.4 periods, and the local fit then settled a bin too high. |0> is the cosine at zero drive and |1> half a period on, whatever amplitude the window starts at; the pi amplitude is `pi / angular_frequency`, the first excited extremum. Uncertainties are propagated from the fit, and are NaN rather than 0 when lmfit cannot estimate the covariance. A flat trace has an undefined (NaN) R².
- `analyze` runs fit -> checks -> save -> plot. A target fails, and nothing reaches the database, when the fit's R² is below the step's minimum (the shared `_require_r2`), when the pi amplitude is undefined or outside the swept window (the sweep stopped short of the excited level, so neither the pi amplitude nor |1> was measured), or when the value the step writes has no uncertainty estimate. The fitted `rabi_amplitude` and `fit_r2` are written to the measurement entry (QHC-1497, QHC-1791).
